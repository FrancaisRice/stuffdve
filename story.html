Two bakers, one bin and a very good idea.

Stuff’d. started behind a neighborhood bakery, where the pretzels left over at closing time kept ending up in the trash.

In 2024, Noor was closing up the bakery and Sam was taking out the trash. The bin was full of pretzels. Good ones. Somebody said, “What if we just stuffed them?” Nobody remembers who.

The first batch was made with a piping bag, a borrowed oven and a lot of cheddar. Friends asked for more. Then their friends did. Within a month we were collecting leftovers from three bakeries on the block.

We learned that dried, milled pretzel makes an incredible dough: deeper, saltier, more pretzel than pretzel. So we stopped thinking of it as a compromise. Upcycling became the recipe.

Three rules we bake by.

Waste is a design flaw.

If something good is being thrown away, the system is broken. We’d rather fix it than accept it.

Delicious comes first.

Nobody buys a snack to save the planet. Upcycled has to taste better, or it doesn’t ship.

Pay the bakers.

Partner bakeries are paid for every kilo we collect. Their leftovers are an ingredient, not a favor.

“It’s what’s on the inside that counts” started as a joke about cheddar. Now it’s the whole company.
